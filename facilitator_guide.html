1. What people should leave with

  • Results mirror Methods, in the same order. Paragraph 1 is participant flow.
  • Each finding has one home: text, table or figure. Then cross-reference.
  • Every estimate has direction, size, a 95% CI and an exact P.
  • They can fix a flawed Table 1 and a flawed figure.
  • For lab data: n/N (%) per drug, breakpoint standard and version, isolates are not patients.
  • They can check an AI-written Results paragraph against its source, and they know that the author stays accountable.
  • They know the best practices for using AI in health-research results: reproducible code, a human in the loop, independent checks, patient privacy, a log and disclosure.

The one slide to photograph: slide 34, "Results self-check".

2. Before the session

The day before

  • Open the deck once on the room laptop. It is one self-contained file, so no internet is needed.
  • Press S to open speaker view. Each slide has a clock time and notes there.
  • Put the PDF on a USB stick and on the desktop as a backup. The PDF has no speaker notes and prints each tab as its own page.
  • Read this guide's "Check before you teach" list.
  • Decide how pairs will form (neighbour to neighbour is fine).

In the room (15 minutes before)

  • Projector shows the full slide, including the footer. Check the back row can read the small text on slides 22, 24, 27 and 28.
  • Clicker or keyboard works. Many slides reveal content on a click.
  • Phone timer or wall clock visible to you. The speaker notes use a clock time, so note your start time.
  • Microphone works for taking answers from the room.

5. Activity 2: fix this table, fix this figure 52–67 min

Slides 23–25. A hypothetical RCT of two surgical dressings. Arms: Dressing A n = 105, Dressing B n = 108.

2a. Fix this table (52–62)

  1. Show slide 23. Say: "Find every problem. Pairs. Three minutes."
  2. Walk the room. Do not click on yet.
  3. Take answers aloud for two minutes. Write them on a board if you have one.
  4. Click to slide 24. Go through the "Errors" tab, then the "Fixed table" tab.

The 8 planted errors
  1. No group n in the header.
  2. P-values in an RCT Table 1 (randomisation handles balance; any difference is by chance).
  3. Inconsistent decimals (11.37, 10.2, 54.2, 55).
  4. A count without a percentage (Male).
  5. A percentage without a count (Diabetes).
  6. Mean ± SD for skewed length of stay. The SD is almost as large as the mean.
  7. "P = 0.000". It should read P < 0.001 (here the whole column goes).
  8. No title, footnotes or abbreviations.

Table 1. Baseline characteristics of participants by surgical dressing

CharacteristicDressing A (n = 105)Dressing B (n = 108)

Check: 61/105 = 58.1%; 58/108 = 53.7%; 31/105 = 29.5%; 29/108 = 26.9%. (The flawed "29.52%" is 31/105 shown to two decimals.)

2b. Fix this figure (62–67)

  1. Show slide 25 on the "Critique" tab. It shows 640 clinical isolates by specimen as an exploded rainbow pie. Ask: "What would you change?" Two minutes.
  2. Click the "Fixed" tab. Compare.
What is wrong with the pie, and the fix
  • Exploded slices and a drop shadow add no information.
  • Rainbow colours with no meaning.
  • Percentages to two decimals (45.94%).
  • A boxed legend that you must look up.
  • An ALL-CAPS title.
  • Five slices of similar size are hard to rank by eye.

Fix: ordered horizontal bars, direct "n (%)" labels, one colour (#1f5560), plain title.

6. Activity 3: catch the AI 70–80 min

Slides 27–29. Cross-sectional study of 312 E. coli urine isolates (first isolate per patient). An AI chatbot was asked to write the Results paragraph from the table. The paragraph has 8 planted errors.

  1. Slide 27 (70–72): give two minutes to read the source table. Point at the "Tested N" column. It is not 312 for every drug.
  2. Slide 28 (72–76): say there are eight errors. Pairs compare the paragraph with the table.
  3. Slide 29 (76–80): click the 8 errors one at a time. Ask who found each. Then show the "Corrected paragraph" tab.

Corrected paragraph
Of the 312 E. coli isolates (first isolate per patient), resistance was highest to ampicillin (271/312, 86.9%) and ciprofloxacin (197/312, 63.1%). Nitrofurantoin resistance was 7.4% (22/298 tested) and fosfomycin resistance 3.8% (9/240 tested). Meropenem resistance was 4.5% (14/312). ESBL production was detected in 158 isolates (50.6%). Prior antibiotic use (aOR 2.4, 95% CI 1.5–3.9; P < 0.001) and hospitalisation in the preceding 3 months (aOR 1.7, 95% CI 1.0–2.9; P = 0.048) were associated with ESBL production.

Debrief line: every one of these errors was fluent and confident. The six-point check on slide 30 catches all eight: trace the numbers (1), check denominators (2), check the unit (4), no causal or "significant" wording (3, 5, 6), no interpretation or citations (7, 8).

Privacy reminder: never upload identifiable patient data to an AI tool.

8. Questions you may get

"A reviewer asked for P-values in Table 1 of my RCT. What do I do?"

CONSORT discourages baseline tests in randomised trials, because any difference is by chance. Follow the journal's instructions. If you decline, give a short reason in the response letter.

"Can I use AI to write my Results section?"

Treat it as a drafting tool for text built from a table you supply. Check every number with the six-point list. Disclose the use following the journal's policy and the ICMJE recommendations. The author is accountable for every word and number. AI is not an author.

"Does the AI tool I use matter?"

The same checks apply to any tool. The errors in Activity 3 are the kind any language model can make.

"We follow EUCAST, our colleagues follow CLSI. Which is right?"

Use the standard your laboratory follows. State which one and which version. Do not mix standards within one table.

"We have only 18 isolates of this organism. Can I give percentages?"

Give n/N. If you give a percentage, add a caveat that the number is small and the estimate is unstable. The slide's rule is: do not report a percentage when N is below 30 without a caveat.

"How do I count repeat isolates from the same patient?"

Count the first isolate per patient per period. This is the convention for cumulative antibiograms (CLSI M39). Repeat isolates inflate resistance.

"So what does this result mean? Why did resistance go up?"

Good question. Meaning belongs in the Discussion, which is covered on Day 2. In Results we report what was found.

10. Check before you teach

These points could not be fully verified when the slides were prepared. Please confirm each one before 23 November.

  • CLSI M39 (cumulative antibiogram) wording on first isolate per patient per period and on the 30-isolate minimum. Check the current edition.
  • CLSI M100 or EUCAST edition and year used in your own laboratory. Slide 22 says "(edition, year)" on purpose.
  • "Walia et al., 2023" on slide 28 is a planted unverified citation. Do not cite it as real.
  • Journal and publisher policies on AI-created or AI-altered images change often. Slide 31 says "many publishers". Check any journal you name.
  • ICMJE wording on AI use and disclosure. Check the current version.
  • Projector test: every slide was checked in a PDF print, not on a projector.
